\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

A logistic regression's disagreement on held-out folds is enough to find most flipped labels under symmetric noise on two small datasets, and acting on it is cheap. The out-of-fold filter is the classical cleansing rule of \citet{brodley1999identifying}. It needs no clean validation set and no noise-rate estimate, so we suggest that work on small, roughly linear problems with symmetric noise report against it. We do not claim it is a good baseline elsewhere.

The filter has clear limits. It discards clean samples: between a quarter and a third of the drop set is correctly labeled (Table~\ref{tab:detection}). On breast cancer at 40\%\src{noise_max_pct} noise it recovers only 22.1\%\src{recovery_bc_n40} of the mean gap, and on both datasets it stays below clean-label accuracy at that rate. Dropped clean samples may be the hard ones, and we did not measure which. A softer rule, such as a confidence threshold, reweighting instead of dropping, or a second filtering round, may close the gap.

The scope is narrow and the study is a measurement for one setting, not a ranking. We study two small datasets, symmetric noise up to 40\%\src{noise_max_pct}, 5\src{n_seeds} seeds, one $K$ and one $C$, neither tuned. The gain over logistic regression at $C=1$ may partly come from regularization strength and training-set size. We did not compare with a smaller or cross-validated $C$, co-teaching \citep{han2018coteaching}, confident learning \citep{northcutt2021confident}, small-loss filtering, or the same filter with another learner, so we make no claim that it beats other noise-robust methods. The MLP is an untuned, unregularized sanity check; an early-stopped MLP and an MLP trained on the filtered set are missing controls. That the MLP degrades faster because it memorizes flipped labels \citep{zhang2016understanding} is a hypothesis we did not test. Class-dependent noise, larger data and deep networks are untested.
