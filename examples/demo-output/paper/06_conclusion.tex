\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}

In our setting, a linear model on small data with symmetric label noise, removing the samples the model disagrees with out of fold gives a clear gain over noisy logistic regression on digits and at 20\%\src{cfg_noise_low_pct} noise on breast cancer, and a small, uncertain gain on breast cancer at 40\%\src{cfg_noise_high_pct}. The filter's explicit setting is the number of folds $K$, its implicit one is $C$, and it costs about $K+1$ base fits.

The study has limits.
We use two small scikit-learn datasets, synthetic symmetric noise and 5\src{n_seeds} seeds that vary the split and the noise together; class-dependent and real noise are untested, and the symmetric case is the one that favors the filter's majority argument.
The baselines are weak, and the comparison with the MLP is secondary. We do not compare with logistic regression at a tuned $C$, with a regularized MLP, with an MLP trained on filtered data, with confident learning \citep{northcutt2021confident}, with Wilson editing or with deep sample-selection methods such as co-teaching \citep{han2018coteaching}. Whether stronger L2 regularization closes much of the gap on digits is open.
We did not vary $K$ or $C$, and we did not measure per-class drop rates.
Roughly a quarter to a third of drops are clean samples, so thresholds or iterated filtering are worth testing.
Variance is high on breast cancer at 40\%\src{cfg_noise_high_pct} noise, where the filter's sd is about 9\src{acc_bc_40_filter_sd} points.
We make no claim that the filter scales to deep networks or large data.
