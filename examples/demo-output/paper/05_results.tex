\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Out-of-fold disagreement flags flipped labels with high precision and recall}

In every noisy cell, most of the samples the out-of-fold filter drops are truly flipped, and precision is well above the flip rate that random dropping would give (Table~\ref{tab:detection}, Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}a). Precision ranges from 64.6\%\src{prec_digits_n20} (digits, 20\%\src{noise_mid_pct} noise) to 76.4\%\src{prec_bc_n20} (breast cancer, 20\%\src{noise_mid_pct}), against sampled flip rates of 19.7\%\src{actual_noise_digits_n20} and 39.4\%\src{actual_noise_digits_n40} on digits and 19.2\%\src{actual_noise_bc_n20} and 38.6\%\src{actual_noise_bc_n40} on breast cancer. The worst single run still reaches 61.1\%\src{prec_min_any}. The filter also catches most flipped labels: recall is 98.5\%\src{rec_digits_n20} and 97.5\%\src{rec_digits_n40} on digits at the two noise rates, and 92.5\%\src{rec_bc_n20} and 78.9\%\src{rec_bc_n40} on breast cancer. The cost is that between a quarter and a third of the dropped samples are clean (Table~\ref{tab:detection}). Digits has ten classes, so a flipped label rarely coincides with the model's wrong class; precision may therefore depend partly on class count, which our two datasets confound.

\subsection{Refitting on the kept samples recovers most of the mean accuracy gap on digits, less on breast cancer}

Noise hurts logistic regression on both datasets: at the highest noise rate, 40\%\src{noise_max_pct}, it loses 13.2\src{loss_digits_n40} points on digits and 16.4\src{loss_bc_n40} points on breast cancer (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The filter beats it on digits at both noise rates and on breast cancer at 40\%\src{noise_max_pct}, and both the confidence intervals and the paired tests exclude zero in these three cells. On breast cancer at 20\%\src{noise_mid_pct} the interval also excludes zero (0.4\src{bc20_logreg_diff_ci_low} to 5.5\src{bc20_logreg_diff_ci_high} points), but the paired test gives $p=0.12$\src{p_bc20_filter_vs_logreg}. With 5\src{n_seeds} seeds the two can disagree, so we treat that gain of 2.9\src{gain_bc_n20_vs_logreg} points as suggestive only. On digits the filter wins back 77.9\%\src{recovery_digits_n20} of the mean gap at 20\%\src{noise_mid_pct} noise and 68.3\%\src{recovery_digits_n40} at 40\%\src{noise_max_pct}.

The limits are clear on breast cancer. The filter recovers 65.8\%\src{recovery_bc_n20} of the mean gap at 20\%\src{noise_mid_pct} noise but only 22.1\%\src{recovery_bc_n40} at 40\%\src{noise_max_pct}, where the seed sd is large (9.0\src{sd_bc_n40_filter} points). The 20\%\src{noise_mid_pct} figure rests on a loss of only 4.4\src{loss_bc_n20} points with a seed sd of 3.7\src{sd_bc_n20_logreg} points, so the ratio is ill-conditioned. At 40\%\src{noise_max_pct} noise the filter stays below clean-label accuracy on both datasets (paired tests against clean logistic regression: $p=0.016$\src{p_dg40_filter_vs_clean} on digits, $p=0.028$\src{p_bc40_filter_vs_clean} on breast cancer). Recovered shares are ratios of means, because per-seed ratios are unstable when the loss is small.

\subsection{On clean labels the filter costs little}

Against logistic regression on clean labels, the filter changes test accuracy by $-0.2$\src{gain_digits_n0_vs_logreg} points on digits and $-0.1$\src{gain_bc_n0_vs_logreg} points on breast cancer (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}c). Neither change is significant, and both intervals include zero (digits $-0.9$\src{dg0_logreg_diff_ci_low} to 0.3\src{dg0_logreg_diff_ci_high}, breast cancer $-0.4$\src{bc0_logreg_diff_ci_low} to 0.0\src{bc0_logreg_diff_ci_high} points). With 5 seeds this rules out a cost of about a point at most on digits, not a zero cost. The filter still drops 3.5\%\src{dropped_frac_digits_n0} (digits) and 1.9\%\src{dropped_frac_bc_n0} (breast cancer) of the clean training samples.

\subsection{Sanity check: an untuned MLP degrades faster than the filter}

At both noisy rates the filter beats the MLP in all 5\src{n_seeds_filter_wins_mlp_digits_n20} seeds of every cell, by 11.0\src{gain_bc_n20_vs_mlp} to 27.7\src{gain_digits_n40_vs_mlp} points (Table~\ref{tab:mlp}, Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}b). This is a check on memorization, not a headline. The MLP is untuned, unregularized and has no early stopping, so it fits the flipped labels. An early-stopped MLP, or an MLP trained on the filtered set, would be the fair comparison, and we did not run either. On clean digits the MLP is ahead of the filter (filter minus MLP $-0.7$\src{gain_digits_n0_vs_mlp} points, paired $p=0.03$\src{p_dg0_filter_vs_mlp}, uncorrected); on clean breast cancer the two are level, with no difference detected ($p=0.70$\src{p_bc0_filter_vs_mlp}).
