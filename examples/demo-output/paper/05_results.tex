\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Out-of-fold disagreement finds most of the flipped labels, and the refit model gains accuracy over plain logistic regression in three of the four noisy cells; the fourth (breast cancer at the lower noise rate) is not significant. Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the accuracies, Table~\ref{tab:drops} the drop statistics, and Figure~\ref{fig:gains} the paired gains.

\subsection{The filter drops mostly flipped labels and finds most of them}

\input{table_drops}

Dropped samples are flipped far more often than the noise rate. A random drop would have a precision equal to the realized noise rate, so Table~\ref{tab:drops} reports precision next to that rate and the lift over it. The lift is large at the lower rate and smaller at the higher rate, and on breast cancer it is smallest, with the lowest recall. Recall is highest on digits. A quarter to a third of dropped samples are clean.

\subsection{Filtering beats logistic regression on digits, with partial support on breast cancer}

Noise costs logistic regression many points, and the cost grows with the noise rate on both datasets (Table~\ref{tab:drops}, loss column). On digits the filter wins at both noise rates in every seed, and its gain grows from the lower to the higher rate (trend test $p$ = 0.0025\src{p_trend_dg_gain_lr}; Figure~\ref{fig:gains}). At the higher rate the gain is 9.0\src{diff_filter_logreg_digits_n40} points.

Breast cancer gives weaker evidence. At the higher rate the filter wins significantly in all seeds, but the seed-to-seed sd in this cell is about 9.0\src{sd_filter_breast_cancer_n40} points. At the lower rate the gain is positive on average but not significant ($p$ = 0.12\src{p_bc20_filter_vs_lr}, one seed does not gain). At the higher rate the filter regains only about a fifth (22\%\src{recovered_breast_cancer_n40}) of the accuracy that noise costs logistic regression (loss column of Table~\ref{tab:drops}), so we make no firm claim for that cell.

\subsection{An untuned MLP reference is hurt more by noisy labels}

The untuned MLP falls much further than either linear model as noise grows, and the filter beats it in all four noisy cells (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:gains}). The gap reaches 28\src{diff_filter_mlp_digits_n40} points on digits at the higher rate, consistent with flexible models fitting wrong labels \citep{zhang2021understanding}. It does not show that filtering beats a well-trained network: the MLP was neither tuned nor stopped early.

\subsection{With clean labels the filter costs less than the test sets can resolve}

With clean labels the filter loses a fraction of a point to logistic regression on both datasets, and neither difference is significant (Table~\ref{tab:main}). One test sample is worth 0.58\src{one_sample_pts_breast_cancer} points on breast cancer, so the cost there is below a single sample and five seeds cannot show equivalence. The cost presumably comes from correctly labeled samples that the filter still discards, about 3.5\%\src{dropped_pct_digits_n0} of the digits training set. The filter takes about five times (5.0\src{time_filter_over_logreg_digits_n0}$\times$) the fit time of logistic regression on digits.
