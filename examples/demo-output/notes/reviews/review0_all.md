# Reviews from review0

## Reviewer: soundness (overall 5/10)

The paper studies a one-pass out-of-fold filter for logistic regression: drop every training sample whose cross-validated prediction disagrees with its given label, then refit. On digits and breast cancer with clean labels and 20% or 40% symmetric noise (5 seeds), it reports that noisy-label drops are mostly mislabeled, that the refit model beats unfiltered logistic regression in 19 of 20 noisy runs while recovering only part of the lost accuracy, and that the cost on clean labels is small. It claims no new method.

- [major] Many claims are verdicts of tests that the paper never shows: no p-value, interval, significance level or statement on multiple comparisons appears. The setup calls the tests descriptive because seeds re-split the same data, yet the text then says what they do and do not 'establish'. Several verdicts are fragile. The gap to the clean-label reference is treated as established on breast cancer at 20% noise (p about 0.049) and as not established on digits at 20% (p about 0.053). A rough reviewer calculation with the corrected resampled t-test for overlapping splits (Nadeau and Bengio, 2003) keeps the gains over logistic regression on digits at both rates and on breast cancer at 40%, but the three 'remains below the clean-label reference' verdicts (p about 0.09 to 0.19) and the 'more than half won back' result on digits at 20% (p about 0.12) no longer pass 0.05. The half-recovery threshold is also never motivated, and the 'Back' ratio has no spread although its denominator can be small relative to the seed-to-seed variation. (where: Sec. 4 'Checks and statistics'; Sec. 4.2 'Recovery is partial'; Sec. 4.3; Fig. 1 caption; Table 1 column 'Back'; Sec. 5). Suggested: Report each paired difference with a 95% interval (a row under Table 2 or in the Fig. 2 caption), state the level and that no multiplicity correction is applied. Either use the corrected resampled test, computable from the per-seed accuracies and split sizes, and revise the affected sentences, or remove the 'establish' language and report means with the number of seeds in each direction. Delete the half-recovery test. Use t-based intervals that match the tests: the 5-sample bootstrap intervals in the notes exclude zero in cases where the t-test does not pass (gain on breast cancer at 20%, half-recovery on digits at 40%).
- [major] The only comparator that bears on filtering is logistic regression at the fixed default C = 1. There is no random-drop or oracle control, no confident learning, no other filter and no tuned regularization. Removing up to about half of the data also changes the effective regularization strength at fixed C, and stronger regularization alone might help the unfiltered baseline under noise. The limitations mention the missing controls but not this confound, and the abstract and contribution list state the gain without the condition on C. (where: Sec. 4 'Methods'; abstract; Sec. 1 third contribution; Sec. 5 'Limitations and next steps'). Suggested: Scope the gain to 'logistic regression with the same fixed C' in the abstract, the contribution and the discussion, and name the regularization confound in the limitations. Add to Table 1 the mislabeled share of the kept set, computable from the saved counts (from seed means roughly 0.4% and 2% on digits and 2% and 15% on breast cancer, against about 20% and 40% before filtering). Random removal would leave that share unchanged in expectation, so this is direct evidence of what the filter does to the training set, while the accuracy control remains future work.
- [major] The paper does not show what is learned beyond its closest antecedent. The abstract of Brodley and Friedl (1999) already reports that single-algorithm and voting filters improve accuracy for noise levels up to 30% and evaluates filter precision (good data thrown away against bad data retained). The paper reports none of these findings, so its stated difference (measuring drops on clean as well as noisy labels) is thin. The motivating gap (no noise rate, no clean set) does not separate the filter from classification filtering or from confident learning. The first contribution presents the filter itself while Sec. 1 says 'We claim no new method', and both the title and the italic central claim are true by construction of any disagreement filter. (where: Title; abstract; Sec. 1 paragraphs 2 and 3 and first contribution; Sec. 2 'Filtering mislabeled samples'). Suggested: State in Sec. 2 what Brodley and Friedl found and which results here confirm or add to it (a linear learner, the 40% rate, the share of lost accuracy won back). Say plainly that prior filters also need neither quantity. Replace the first contribution with a measurement, and move the title and the italic claim to the empirical finding (mostly mislabeled drops with partial recovery).
- [minor] The reader is never told that breast cancer is a binary task and digits a ten-class task. Symmetric noise at 40% means very different things in the two cases: in the binary case labels are close to uninformative (50%). This is central to reading the weak case and the large spread. In the saved runs, the two seeds with the most flips on breast cancer at 40% are also the two least accurate for both logistic regression and the filter. The noise draws are also nested across rates for a given seed (same generator seed and threshold rule), which matters for the comparisons between rates and is not stated. (where: Sec. 4 'Data and noise'; Sec. 4.1 'Heavier noise shifts what is dropped'; Sec. 4.2). Suggested: State the number of classes in words, note that breast cancer is imbalanced and that 40% binary noise approaches the uninformative point, mention the nested flips in the setup, and, with a recipe, note that the spread on breast cancer coincides with the realized flip share.
- [minor] Comparing drop precision between noise rates mixes filter quality with prevalence. 'Precision rises on digits and falls on breast cancer' reads as better selection on digits, but relative to the flipped share the selection weakens on both datasets (from seed means, precision over flipped share goes from about 3.3 to 1.9 on digits and from 4.0 to 1.8 on breast cancer). The share of correctly labeled samples that are dropped rises on both (about 13% to 20% and 7% to 23%). (where: Sec. 4.1 'Heavier noise shifts what is dropped'; Table 1). Suggested: Report the share of correctly labeled samples dropped (a column in Table 1, computable from the saved counts) and rewrite the paragraph around it and drop recall. This also quantifies the 'price' that is now only called a sizable minority.
- [minor] 'Removal adapts to the labels' invites reading the drop share as calibrated to the noise rate. The number of disagreements is expected to grow with noise, and Table 1 shows that the drop share exceeds the flipped share in every noisy setting. (where: Sec. 4.1 'Removal adapts to the labels'; Sec. 1 italic claim). Suggested: Say that the filter removes more as noise grows and consistently removes more than the flipped share.
- [minor] 'Costs little' on clean labels rests on two mean differences and a non-significant test, which the text itself says does not show equivalence. The reader gets no bound on the cost. 'Every drop is correctly labeled' also assumes the original labels are error-free, although the setup says existing errors are not counted. (where: Sec. 4.3; Sec. 4.1 first paragraph). Suggested: Give the per-seed range or a 95% interval (per seed, the difference runs from about -1.3 to +0.6 points on digits and from -0.6 to 0 on breast cancer), and call these drops 'unflipped'.
- [minor] Space goes to repeated or uninformative content while the statistics are missing. Accuracy appears three times (Fig. 1c, Table 2, Fig. 2) and drop composition twice (Fig. 1b, Table 1). The untuned MLP takes a setup description, a table row and a paragraph whose conclusion is that the comparison is not evidence. The bars in Fig. 1c start well above zero, which exaggerates height ratios. (where: Fig. 1b and 1c; Fig. 2; Table 2; Sec. 4.2 'The MLP gap does not isolate filtering'). Suggested: Cut the MLP paragraph to one or two sentences, replace the bars in Fig. 1c with points or shrink the panel, and use the room for paired differences with intervals and the added Table 1 columns.
- [minor] The same caveats ('the seeds do not establish', 'on seed means') recur in the abstract, both figure captions, Sec. 4.2 and Sec. 5. The Fig. 1 caption is very long and 'Recovery is partial' is hard to parse, so the reader loses which results hold. (where: Abstract; Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 captions; Sec. 4.2; Sec. 5). Suggested: State each uncertainty once, next to its interval in Sec. 4.2, keep captions to how to read the figure and what to notice, and organize 'Recovery is partial' as three settings that recover much and one that recovers little.
- [minor] Two figure encodings are unclear: the legend entry 'All mislabeled' can be read as 'all samples are mislabeled', and the red zero line in Fig. 2 is not explained in the figure or its caption. (where: Fig. 1b legend; Fig. 2). Suggested: Rename the entry to 'Flipped share' and add to the Fig. 2 caption that the red line at zero marks logistic regression.
- [minor] The experimental setup contains a sentence about the agent pipeline used to recompute numbers and check references, with a software citation that names its authors. It tells the reader nothing about the experiment, and it sits awkwardly in an anonymous submission. (where: Sec. 4 'Checks and statistics', first sentence). Suggested: Remove it from the setup. If a tool statement is required, place it where the venue allows such statements, after checking anonymity rules.
- [question] How does the filter differ in procedure from the single-algorithm filter of Brodley and Friedl, apart from the choice of logistic regression and the noise rates tested?
- [question] Are out-of-fold predictions needed for a linear model with these sample sizes? No in-sample filter was run, so the rationale in Sec. 3 about memorized labels is a design argument rather than a measured result.
- [question] Does the filter remove some classes more than others, in particular on breast cancer at 40% noise? The saved records hold only total drop counts.
- [question] Which of the significance verdicts do the authors consider part of the paper's claims, given that Sec. 4 calls the tests descriptive?
- [question] How were K = 5, C = 1 and the MLP settings chosen, and were any other values tried before these results?

## Reviewer: clarity (overall 5/10)

The paper studies a one-pass filter that drops every training sample whose out-of-fold logistic regression prediction disagrees with its given label, then refits on the rest. On digits and breast cancer with clean labels and two rates of symmetric noise, it reports that most drops under noise are mislabeled, that the refit model beats unfiltered logistic regression in all noisy runs but one and costs little on clean labels, and that the lost accuracy is only partly recovered.

- [major] The compiled PDF loses its bold and italic emphasis and is set in a substitute font instead of Times. Run-in heads, the contribution lead-ins, the italicized central claim and the bold best values in Table 2 all print in upright regular weight. The Table 2 caption says that bold marks the best mean, but no value is bold on the page, so paragraphs and winners are hard to find. (where: main.pdf, all pages (Sec. 1 contribution list, run-in heads in Secs. 2 to 5, Table 2); paper/main.log reports that TU/ptm/b/n and TU/ptm/m/it are undefined and substituted). Suggested: Compile with pdfLaTeX, or load a Times font that XeTeX can find (for example TeX Gyre Termes through fontspec, guarded by an engine test) in the main.tex preamble without editing cvpr.sty. Then render the PDF again, confirm that bold and italic appear, and recheck the page limit because line breaks will change.
- [major] The word 'establish' carries most of the caveats, but the criterion is never defined and no p-value or interval is printed. A reader cannot tell why the gap to the clean-label reference counts on breast cancer at 20% noise (95.2 vs. 96.7) but not on digits at 20% noise (95.1 vs. 96.8). The two p-values in the notes sit just on either side of 0.05 (0.049 and 0.053). The sentence that describes the tests ('paired between methods, noise rates or two shares from the same run, or compare a share with one half') cannot be followed on a first read. (where: Sec. 4, paragraph 'Checks and statistics'; Fig. 1 caption; Sec. 4.2; Fig. 2 caption; Sec. 5). Suggested: Define the term once, for example 'We call a difference established when a two-sided paired t-test across seeds gives p < 0.05.' Mark established differences in Table 2 or in the figure with a symbol, or print the existing p-value recipes next to the borderline cases and say that they are borderline. Replace the pairing sentence with a plain one that says which two quantities each reported comparison pairs.
- [major] The sentence 'The refit model remains below the clean-label reference at the higher rate on both datasets and on breast cancer at the lower rate' reads as if the filter reaches the reference on digits at the lower rate. Table 2 shows that its mean is lower there too. The omission reflects a test result that the sentence does not mention. (where: Sec. 4.2, paragraph 'Recovery is partial', last sentence). Suggested: Write that the mean of the refit model stays below the clean-label reference in every noisy setting, and that the tests do not establish this gap on digits at the lower rate.
- [major] The results prose chains qualified comparisons and often leaves the reader without a takeaway. The sentence that starts 'Only on digits at the lower rate does a paired test establish that more than half is won back' tests a one-half threshold that is never motivated. 'Our advantage over this baseline is larger at the higher rate than with clean labels' compares against a clean-label gain that is close to zero, which says little. The paragraph 'Heavier noise shifts what is dropped' reports mixed directions and ends by saying that no cause is shown. (where: Sec. 4.1, paragraph 'Heavier noise shifts what is dropped'; Sec. 4.2, paragraphs 'Filtering mostly beats training on all noisy labels' and 'Recovery is partial'). Suggested: Open each paragraph with one finding and keep only the evidence for it. Cut the half-recovery sentence (the Back column already shows the shares) and the comparison between the higher rate and clean labels. Reduce the 'Heavier noise' paragraph to one sentence about breast cancer at the higher rate inside 'Recovery is partial'.
- [major] The same results appear several times in a 4-page paper. Removed and mislabeled shares are in Fig. 1(b) and Table 1. Accuracy under noise is in Fig. 1(c), Fig. 2 and Table 2, and again as a recovered share in Table 1. The reader has to work out whether each display adds anything, while the space is needed for the test criterion, the recovery formula and basic dataset facts. Fig. 1(c) is also the weakest of the three accuracy views: its bars sit on a truncated axis, and error bars, seed dots and the reference line crowd each other. (where: Fig. 1(b,c), Fig. 2, Table 1, Table 2). Suggested: Keep two accuracy views. For example, replace the bars in Fig. 1(c) with the paired-gain plot of Fig. 2 and delete Fig. 2. This removes the truncated axis, shows the losing run on page 2 and keeps Table 2 for absolute accuracy. Use the freed space for the items listed in the other points.
- [major] The introduction does not say which question the paper answers or what is new. The gap paragraph is one sentence about repairs that need corruption probabilities or the noise rate. The next paragraph says the filter belongs to classification filtering and claims no new method. The first contribution, 'A filter that needs only the noisy training set. We describe...', then reads as a method contribution, and the third bullet is one long sentence with nested exceptions. (where: Sec. 1, paragraphs 2 and 3 and the contribution list). Suggested: State the question in paragraph 2: what a plain out-of-fold filter removes, gains and costs for logistic regression on clean and symmetrically noisy labels, and where it falls short. Recast the first bullet around the study (drops checked against known flips, on clean as well as noisy labels) instead of the filter itself. Shorten the third bullet to the main result and its one weak case.
- [minor] Names drift. The method appears as 'ours', 'Filter (ours)', 'the filter', 'the out-of-fold filter' and 'the refit model'. The baseline appears as 'logistic regression', 'unfiltered logistic regression', 'logistic regression trained on all noisy labels', 'noisy-label logistic regression' and 'this baseline'. (where: Abstract, Secs. 1, 4 and 5, Fig. 1(c) legend, Fig. 2 y label, Table 2). Suggested: Pick one name for the method and one for the baseline and use them in text, legends, axis labels and tables. ## Reviewer: significance (overall 4/10)

The paper studies a one-pass filter that drops every training sample whose out-of-fold logistic regression prediction disagrees with its given label and then refits, and it states that this is classification filtering and not a new method. On digits and breast cancer with symmetric noise, the refit model beats unfiltered logistic regression in 19 of 20 noisy runs, loses little on clean labels, drops mostly mislabeled samples, and recovers much of the lost accuracy except on breast cancer at 40% noise.

- [major] What a reader learns beyond the 1999 prior work is not established. The filter is the single-algorithm classification filter of Brodley and Friedl. Their abstract already reports that filtering improves accuracy for noise levels up to 30% and evaluates how much good data is discarded and how much bad data is kept. Both headline results here (accuracy gain and what is removed) therefore have a direct antecedent. The stated difference, 'We measure what it drops on clean as well as noisy labels', does not tell the reader which finding is new. To my knowledge their learners also included a linear one, which the authors should check. (where: Sec. 2, paragraph 'Filtering mislabeled samples'; Sec. 1, third paragraph and contribution list). Suggested: After reading the paper by Brodley and Friedl, state in two or three sentences what they found (including filter precision) and what this study adds, for example symmetric noise at 40%, per-run precision and recall for logistic regression, or the residual noise analysis proposed below. Frame the paper openly as a small measurement or replication study. Merge the first contribution ('A filter that needs only the noisy training set') into the setup, since describing a known filter is not a contribution.
- [major] The gap that motivates the work does not hold as written. The second introduction paragraph contrasts the filter with methods that use corruption probabilities or the noise rate, yet it says these are then estimated, so the practitioner does not have to know them. Classification filtering already needs neither a noise rate nor a clean set, and the paper presents confident learning as a close relative that estimates what it needs from the data. Still, 'without knowing the noise rate' carries the title, the third abstract sentence, the italic central claim and the first contribution. (where: Title; abstract; Sec. 1, second and third paragraphs). Suggested: Replace the gap with the question the data can answer: how much a single linear out-of-fold filter recovers on small data, what it removes, and where it fails. Change the title to name that question and its scope (small datasets, symmetric noise). Keep the 'needs no noise rate' property as one sentence in Sec. 3, and italicize a measured finding in the introduction instead of a property of the design.
- [major] The most informative result available from the runs is absent: how noisy the training set still is after filtering. From the seed means, the flipped share among kept samples is roughly 0.4% and 2% on digits at 20% and 40% noise, and roughly 2% and 15% on breast cancer. This says directly what the filter achieves and shows that breast cancer at 40% is left with far more noise than the other settings. Precision against one half and recall do not let a reader see this. The small residual noise on digits at 40%, next to a remaining accuracy gap, also suggests that the two datasets fall short for different reasons. (where: Sec. 4.1, Table 1, Fig. 1b; Sec. 4.2, paragraph 'Recovery is partial'). Suggested: Add a recipe computing (n_noisy - n_dropped_noisy) / (n_train - n_dropped) per seed from results/runs.jsonl and print it as a 'Noise left' column in Table 1 beside 'Flip.'. Add the share of correctly labeled samples that are discarded (from the means, about 20% on digits and 23% on breast cancer at 40%), which makes 'a sizable minority' concrete. Build Sec. 4.1 around these two quantities. Present the link to lost clean samples on digits as a hypothesis, since there is no control for it.
- [major] The reader cannot tell when the finding should transfer. The paper never says that one task has ten classes and the other two, or what the inputs are. Symmetric 40% noise in a binary task is close to the point where labels carry no information, whereas with ten classes the true label remains far more frequent than any wrong one. This is a plausible reason for the different behavior at 40%. Both clean-label references are also near 97%, so the out-of-fold model is tested only where a linear model fits very well. Nothing ties the study to a vision problem a workshop attendee has. (where: Sec. 4, 'Data and noise'; Sec. 4.2; Sec. 5). Suggested: State in words the number of classes and the input type of each dataset, and discuss what that implies at 40% noise. Say that the filter depends on an accurate out-of-fold model and was tested only on tasks where the clean linear model is very accurate. Use the saved runs to show that on breast cancer at 40% the two seeds with the most flipped labels are also the two where both unfiltered and filtered models score lowest (descriptive, five seeds). In the introduction, name linear probes on pretrained image features as a motivating use and say clearly that it was not tested.
- [major] No other treatment of label noise is compared, so the size of the gain cannot be judged. The baselines are no treatment and an untuned MLP that the paper itself says does not isolate filtering. Confident learning, voting filters, a noise-tolerant logistic regression and simply stronger regularization (C is fixed at 1) are all cheap alternatives for this base model. The limitation appears only at the end, while the title and contributions read as an endorsement of this filter. (where: Sec. 4 'Methods'; Sec. 4.2 'The MLP gap does not isolate filtering'; Sec. 5). Suggested: Say in the introduction that the only comparison that tests filtering is against the same model without it, and that no alternative noise treatment was run. Add the choice of C to the limitations and next steps. Cut the MLP paragraph to one or two sentences and use the space for the residual noise analysis. If the MLP stays, relate it to the verified report in northcutt2021pervasive that lower-capacity models may be more useful when many labels are wrong.
- [minor] The same results are shown repeatedly in a four-page paper. Fig. 1b repeats the Flip., Drop and Prec. columns of Table 1, and the comparison of the filter with logistic regression appears in Fig. 1c, Table 2 and Fig. 2. Fig. 1c also uses bars on an axis that does not start at zero, which inflates the visual gaps. (where: Fig. 1b-c, Table 1, Table 2, Fig. 2). Suggested: Keep one accuracy figure and the accuracy table. For example, move the paired-gain plot, which has a true zero, into Fig. 1c and delete the separate Fig. 2. Use the freed space for the comparison with prior work and the 'Noise left' analysis.
- [minor] Statistical caveats crowd out the takeaways. Variants of 'the seeds do not establish' recur in both figure captions, Sec. 4.2 and Sec. 5, yet no p value or interval is printed, and Sec. 4 calls the tests descriptive. The test of whether more than half of the loss is won back uses a threshold that no reader needs. (where: Fig. 1 caption, Fig. 2 caption, Sec. 4.2, Sec. 5). Suggested: Mark in Table 2 which paired differences pass the test, or print the 95% intervals already available, and state the weak case once in the text. Delete the sentences on the one-half threshold. Shorten the Fig. 1 caption to what is needed to read the panels.
- [minor] The comparison of drop precision across noise rates mixes in the change of base rate. Precision rising on digits does not mean the filter selects better at 40%: relative to the flipped share, enrichment falls on both datasets (about 3.3 to 1.9 times on digits and 4.0 to 1.8 times on breast cancer). Likewise, 'Removal adapts to the labels' follows largely from the design, since flipped labels create disagreements, and the removed share exceeds the flipped share, so it is not an estimate of the noise rate. (where: Sec. 4.1, paragraphs 'Removal adapts to the labels' and 'Heavier noise shifts what is dropped'). Suggested: Rewrite the last paragraph of Sec. 4.1 in terms of the noise left after filtering and the share of correct labels lost. State that the removed share grows with noise but overshoots the flipped share.
- [minor] Placement among related filters is thin where it matters most. A full paragraph covers sample selection for deep networks, while other data filters and noise-tolerant logistic regression, the nearest alternatives for this setting, are missing. Several references also point to preprints or give no venue (co-teaching, DivideMix, scikit-learn). (where: Sec. 2; references). Suggested: Shorten the deep-network paragraph and add one or two sentences citing the verified keys saez2016inffc, wilson1972asymptotic, verbaeten2003ensemble and bootkrajang2012labelnoise, keeping to what was verified for each (title only for the last two). Cite the published venues: NeurIPS 2018 for co-teaching, ICLR 2020 for DivideMix and JMLR 2011 for scikit-learn.
- [minor] The first sentences of 'Checks and statistics' report that numbers and references were checked with a paper-writing agent pipeline. This does not help a reader judge the method or the evidence, takes space in a short paper and draws attention away from the study. (where: Sec. 4, 'Checks and statistics', first sentence and its reference). Suggested: Remove the sentence and the citation from the experiments section. If the venue requires a disclosure of tool use, place it where the venue policy asks for it.
- [question] Did Brodley and Friedl evaluate a linear learner on its own and a zero-noise condition? Which findings here differ from theirs?

## Reviewer: experiments (overall 4/10)

The paper studies a one-pass filter that drops each training sample whose out-of-fold logistic regression prediction disagrees with its given label, then refits. On digits and breast cancer, with clean labels and two rates of symmetric noise over five seeds, the refit model beats unfiltered logistic regression in all noisy runs but one, drops mostly mislabeled samples under noise and loses little on clean labels, with weak recovery on breast cancer at the higher rate. The authors claim no new method, and they run no random-drop, oracle or competing noisy-label filtering baseline.

- [major] The experiments cannot show that the gain comes from choosing the right samples, or that the filter is preferable to any other cheap remedy. The only comparison that isolates filtering is against unfiltered logistic regression at a fixed C = 1. There is no random-drop or oracle control, no thresholded confident learning or voting filter, and no check of whether stronger regularization alone narrows the gap. The MLP does not fill this role. The missing controls surface only in the last paragraph, after the abstract and contributions have already framed the gain as accuracy 'won back'. (where: Abstract; Sec. 1, third contribution; Sec. 4, 'Methods'; Sec. 5, 'Limitations and next steps'). Suggested: State in the 'Methods' paragraph that the single control for filtering is logistic regression at the same fixed C, and tie the recovery claim to that baseline ('at fixed C'). Use the chance reference the data already supply for selection quality: removing the same number of samples at random has an expected precision equal to Flip and an expected recall equal to Drop, so say this when introducing Prec. and Rec. in Table 1. Add that stronger regularization was not tried to the limitations, and move the sentence on not attributing the gain to removing the right samples next to the accuracy result in Sec. 4.2.
- [major] The placement against prior work leaves the contribution unclear. The first contribution presents the filter itself, while the paragraph above says no new method is claimed. The gap in the introduction (other repairs need corruption probabilities or noise rates 'which then have to be estimated') does not separate this work from its closest relatives, since such quantities can be estimated from the noisy data, and confident learning also needs no clean set and no known noise rate. To my knowledge, the confident learning paper itself evaluates the argmax-disagreement rule as one of its variants (the confusion-matrix method); please check the source. (where: Sec. 1, paragraphs 2 and 3 and first contribution; Sec. 2, 'Filtering mislabeled samples'). Suggested: Replace the first contribution with a measurement contribution (what an existing single-learner classification filter removes, recovers and costs for a linear model on clean and noisy labels). In Sec. 2, say exactly which published rule this equals, after verifying it. Rewrite the second paragraph of the introduction around the unknown that the study answers, not around inputs that other methods need.
- [major] The most direct measure of whether filtering works, the share of mislabeled samples left in the kept set, is never reported, and the main failure is described but not examined. From results/runs.jsonl (my approximate computation, mean of per-seed ratios), the kept set is about 0.4% and 2% mislabeled on digits at the lower and higher rates, about 2% on breast cancer at the lower rate and about 15% on breast cancer at the higher rate. In that last setting, the two seeds that end near 74% accuracy are also the two with the highest realized noise rate (about 42% against 35 to 38%) and the most residual noise (about 18 to 24% against 8 to 13%). This is a much stronger and more informative result than what the text offers ('Accuracy there varies widely between seeds'; 'Our runs do not show that one causes the other'). (where: Sec. 4.1, Table 1; Sec. 4.2, 'Recovery is partial'; Fig. 1c, breast cancer at 40%). Suggested: Add a column to Table 1 with the mislabeled share of the kept set, next to Flip., with recipes from the existing runs. Add two sentences on breast cancer at the higher rate that report the realized noise rate and residual noise of the low and high seeds, worded as an association across five seeds. The space can come from the duplicated figures and the MLP paragraph.
- [major] The statistics cannot be checked and their binary use misleads. The text repeatedly says that the seeds do or do not 'establish' a result, but no significance level, p-value or interval appears anywhere. The list of settings that remain below the clean-label reference omits digits at the lower rate, although its mean gap in Table 2 is slightly larger than that on breast cancer at the lower rate, which is listed (the two p-values in the project notes sit just on either side of 0.05). Breast cancer at the lower rate is counted among the settings that win back much of the lost accuracy although the gain itself is not established there, and from the raw runs its per-seed recovered share ranges from below zero to 100%. The test of whether the share exceeds one half has no stated motivation. (where: Sec. 4, 'Checks and statistics'; Sec. 4.2, first two paragraphs; abstract; captions of Figs. 1 and 2). Suggested: State the significance level and whether any multiple-comparison correction was applied. Report the paired difference with sd and p-value (or interval) as an extra row in Table 2 or in the Fig. 2 caption. Drop the one-half tests. Describe breast cancer at the lower rate as a higher mean that is unresolved, with an unstable recovered share, in the abstract, contributions and Sec. 4.2. Report both lower-rate gaps to the clean-label reference in the same way.
- [minor] The paragraph on how heavier noise shifts the drops compares precision across settings whose share of flipped labels doubles. Precision rising on digits therefore does not mean that selection improved: relative to the flipped share it falls. The table values also imply that the share of correct samples discarded grows from the lower to the higher rate on both datasets (roughly 13 to 20% on digits and 7 to 23% on breast cancer, my computation from the recorded drop shares), which is the clearer finding and is not reported. (where: Sec. 4.1, 'Heavier noise shifts what is dropped'). Suggested: Report the share of correctly labeled samples that are dropped next to recall, and say that at the higher rate the filter both misses more flips and discards a larger share of the correct samples. Discuss precision only relative to Flip. Also note that Drop exceeds Flip in every noisy row, which states the 'price' directly.
- [minor] The setup omits the number of classes and features. Digits is a ten-class image task and breast cancer a binary tabular task. This matters for reading symmetric noise: at 40% a binary label carries little information, while in ten classes the wrong labels spread over nine classes. A reader needs this to interpret the one clear weak case and the low MLP accuracy there. (where: Sec. 4, 'Data and noise'). Suggested: Add classes and features for both datasets (with recipes or a cited dataset description), say that digits is the only image dataset, and mention the binary case when discussing the higher rate on breast cancer without presenting it as a proven cause.
