# Claim ledger

The paper may make only these claims, at this scope. Strong claims can be stated directly; weak claims are stated with their limits; unsupported claims are never made.

- **filter_method** (method, strong): The filter fits logistic regression with 5-fold cross-validation on the noisy labels, drops every training sample whose out-of-fold prediction disagrees with its given label, and refits on the rest. It needs no clean validation set and the code never uses a noise rate.  
  Scope: method description; linear model; symmetric noise setting. Evidence: `filter_folds`, `logreg_C`.
- **setup_scope** (scope, strong): The study is small: two scikit-learn datasets (digits, breast cancer), symmetric label noise at 0, 20 and 40%, five seeds, a logistic regression and one 64-unit MLP.  
  Scope: whole study. Evidence: `n_datasets`, `n_noise_rates`, `n_seeds`, `n_runs`, `mlp_hidden`, `mlp_max_iter`, `n_train_digits`, `n_train_breast_cancer`.
- **noise_hurts_logreg** (result, strong): Symmetric label noise costs plain logistic regression many accuracy points, and the cost grows with the noise rate on both datasets.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 20 and 40% symmetric noise; mean over 5 seeds, relative to logreg at 0% noise. Evidence: `loss_logreg_digits_n20`, `loss_logreg_digits_n40`, `loss_logreg_breast_cancer_n20`, `loss_logreg_breast_cancer_n40`, `acc_logreg_digits_n0`, `acc_logreg_breast_cancer_n0`.
- **filter_beats_logreg_digits** (comparison, strong): On digits, the filter beats plain logistic regression at both 20% and 40% noise, and the gain is larger at 40%.  
  Scope: digits; 20% and 40% symmetric noise; paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_logreg_digits_n20`, `diff_filter_logreg_digits_n40`, `acc_filter_digits_n20`, `acc_logreg_digits_n20`, `acc_filter_digits_n40`, `acc_logreg_digits_n40`, `dg20_lr_diff_ci_low`, `dg20_lr_diff_ci_high`, `dg40_lr_diff_ci_low`, `dg40_lr_diff_ci_high`, `p_dg20_filter_vs_lr`, `p_dg40_filter_vs_lr`.
- **filter_beats_logreg_bc_high** (comparison, strong): On breast cancer, the filter beats plain logistic regression at 40% noise.  
  Scope: breast cancer; 40% symmetric noise; paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_logreg_breast_cancer_n40`, `acc_filter_breast_cancer_n40`, `acc_logreg_breast_cancer_n40`, `bc40_lr_diff_ci_low`, `bc40_lr_diff_ci_high`, `p_bc40_filter_vs_lr`.
- **filter_vs_logreg_bc_moderate** (limitation, weak): On breast cancer at 20% noise, the filter's gain over logistic regression is positive on average but not significant with 5 seeds.  
  Scope: breast cancer; 20% symmetric noise; paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_logreg_breast_cancer_n20`, `bc20_lr_diff_ci_low`, `bc20_lr_diff_ci_high`, `dz_bc20_filter_vs_lr`, `p_bc20_filter_vs_lr`.
- **filter_wins_most_noisy_runs** (result, strong): Across all noisy runs the filter beats plain logistic regression in nearly every paired run and beats the MLP in every one.  
  Scope: both datasets; 20% and 40% noise; 20 paired (dataset, noise, seed) runs. Evidence: `nwins_filter_logreg_noisy`, `nwins_filter_mlp_noisy`.
- **filter_beats_mlp_noisy** (comparison, strong): Under label noise, the filter beats an untuned MLP trained on the same noisy labels on both datasets, with gaps of 11 to 28 points that grow with the noise rate.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 20% and 40% symmetric noise; MLP with 64 hidden units, 500 iterations, untuned; paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_mlp_digits_n20`, `diff_filter_mlp_digits_n40`, `diff_filter_mlp_breast_cancer_n20`, `diff_filter_mlp_breast_cancer_n40`, `acc_mlp_digits_n20`, `acc_mlp_digits_n40`, `acc_mlp_breast_cancer_n20`, `acc_mlp_breast_cancer_n40`, `dg20_mlp_diff_ci_low`, `dg40_mlp_diff_ci_low`, `bc20_mlp_diff_ci_low`, `bc40_mlp_diff_ci_low`, `p_dg20_filter_vs_mlp`, `p_dg40_filter_vs_mlp`, `p_bc20_filter_vs_mlp`, `p_bc40_filter_vs_mlp`.
- **mlp_memorizes_noise** (result, weak): The MLP is hurt far more by label noise than the linear model, falling to about 65% accuracy at 40% noise on both datasets; this is consistent with a flexible model fitting the wrong labels, but no experiment tests that explanation.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 0 to 40% noise; untuned MLP. Evidence: `acc_mlp_digits_n0`, `acc_mlp_digits_n40`, `acc_mlp_breast_cancer_n0`, `acc_mlp_breast_cancer_n40`, `acc_logreg_digits_n40`, `acc_logreg_breast_cancer_n40`.
- **filter_drops_mostly_mislabeled** (result, strong): Most samples the filter drops are truly mislabeled: mean precision is above 50% in all four noisy cells (about 65 to 76%).  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 20% and 40% symmetric noise; mean over 5 seeds; one-sample t-test against 50%. Evidence: `precision_digits_n20`, `precision_digits_n40`, `precision_breast_cancer_n20`, `precision_breast_cancer_n40`, `precision_pooled_noisy`, `precision_min_digits_n40`, `precision_min_breast_cancer_n40`, `p_prec_dg20_gt50`, `p_prec_dg40_gt50`, `p_prec_bc20_gt50`, `p_prec_bc40_gt50`.
- **filter_recall_high** (result, strong): The filter removes most of the flipped labels: mean recall is 79 to 98% across the noisy cells, and highest on digits.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 20% and 40% noise; mean over 5 seeds. Evidence: `recall_digits_n20`, `recall_digits_n40`, `recall_breast_cancer_n20`, `recall_breast_cancer_n40`, `dropped_digits_n20`, `dropped_digits_n40`, `dropped_breast_cancer_n20`, `dropped_breast_cancer_n40`.
- **filter_recovers_most_loss_partial** (result, weak): The filter regains most of the accuracy that noise costs logistic regression at 20% noise on both datasets and at 40% noise on digits (about 66 to 78% of the loss), but only about 22% on breast cancer at 40% noise.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 20% and 40% noise; recovered share = (filter - logreg) / (logreg at 0% - logreg), means over 5 seeds; clean reference is logreg at 0% noise, not a clean-label oracle. Evidence: `recovered_digits_n20`, `recovered_digits_n40`, `recovered_breast_cancer_n20`, `recovered_breast_cancer_n40`, `acc_filter_digits_n40`, `acc_filter_breast_cancer_n40`, `sd_filter_breast_cancer_n40`.
- **gain_grows_with_noise_digits** (trend, strong): On digits, the filter's gain over logistic regression and over the MLP grows from 20% to 40% noise.  
  Scope: digits; 20% vs 40% symmetric noise; per-seed gain, paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_logreg_digits_n20`, `diff_filter_logreg_digits_n40`, `diff_filter_mlp_digits_n20`, `diff_filter_mlp_digits_n40`, `trend_dg_gain_lr_ci_low`, `trend_dg_gain_lr_ci_high`, `p_trend_dg_gain_lr`, `p_trend_dg_gain_mlp`.
- **gain_grows_with_noise_bc** (trend, weak): On breast cancer, the filter's gain also tends to rise from 20% to 40% noise, but the increase is not significant against logistic regression (p = 0.66) or the MLP (p = 0.053).  
  Scope: breast cancer; 20% vs 40% noise; per-seed gain, paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_logreg_breast_cancer_n20`, `diff_filter_logreg_breast_cancer_n40`, `diff_filter_mlp_breast_cancer_n20`, `diff_filter_mlp_breast_cancer_n40`, `trend_bc_gain_lr_ci_low`, `trend_bc_gain_lr_ci_high`, `p_trend_bc_gain_lr`, `p_trend_bc_gain_mlp`.
- **clean_cost_small** (result, weak): With clean labels the filter loses at most about 0.2 points of accuracy relative to logistic regression, and the difference is not statistically detectable.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 0% noise; paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `cost_clean_digits`, `cost_clean_breast_cancer`, `acc_filter_digits_n0`, `acc_logreg_digits_n0`, `acc_filter_breast_cancer_n0`, `acc_logreg_breast_cancer_n0`, `dg0_lr_diff_ci_low`, `dg0_lr_diff_ci_high`, `bc0_lr_diff_ci_low`, `bc0_lr_diff_ci_high`, `nwins_filter_logreg_clean`, `p_dg0_filter_vs_lr`, `p_bc0_filter_vs_lr`.
- **clean_drops** (result, strong): On clean labels the filter still discards a few training samples, all of them correctly labeled: about 3.5% of digits and 1.9% of breast cancer training data.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 0% noise; mean over 5 seeds. Evidence: `dropped_digits_n0`, `dropped_breast_cancer_n0`, `dropped_pct_digits_n0`, `dropped_pct_breast_cancer_n0`.
- **mlp_ties_clean** (limitation, strong): With clean labels the untuned MLP is as good as or slightly better than the filter: the gap is not significant on breast cancer and is under one point in the MLP's favor on digits.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; 0% noise; paired over 5 seeds. Evidence: `diff_filter_mlp_digits_n0`, `diff_filter_mlp_breast_cancer_n0`, `acc_mlp_digits_n0`, `acc_filter_digits_n0`, `dg0_mlp_diff_ci_low`, `dg0_mlp_diff_ci_high`, `bc0_mlp_diff_ci_low`, `bc0_mlp_diff_ci_high`, `p_dg0_filter_vs_mlp`, `p_bc0_filter_vs_mlp`.
- **filter_variance_bc_high_noise** (limitation, strong): Breast cancer at 40% noise is unstable: both the filter and logistic regression vary by about 9 points across seeds, so no tight claim is made for that cell.  
  Scope: breast cancer; 40% noise; 5 seeds. Evidence: `sd_filter_breast_cancer_n40`, `sd_logreg_breast_cancer_n40`, `sd_mlp_breast_cancer_n40`, `noisy_pct_breast_cancer_n40`.
- **filter_cost_time** (limitation, weak): The filter costs about five times the fit time of logistic regression on digits, because of the cross-validation and the refit (indicative; hardware unknown).  
  Scope: digits; 0% noise; wall-clock fit seconds. Evidence: `time_filter_over_logreg_digits_n0`, `filter_folds`.
- **no_baselines_no_scaling** (limitation, strong): We do not compare against co-teaching or confident learning, run no ablations (folds, C, MLP size), and make no claim about deep networks, large data, or noise types other than symmetric flips.  
  Scope: whole study. Evidence: `n_datasets`, `n_seeds`, `noise_high_pct`, `mlp_hidden`.
- **contribution_filter_study** (contribution, strong): We present a simple out-of-fold disagreement filter for linear classifiers and show on two small datasets that it recovers much of the accuracy lost to symmetric label noise, drops mostly mislabeled samples, beats an untuned MLP, and costs little on clean labels.  
  Scope: digits, breast cancer; symmetric noise 0 to 40%; logistic regression. Evidence: `diff_filter_logreg_digits_n40`, `precision_pooled_noisy`, `diff_filter_mlp_digits_n40`, `cost_clean_digits`, `p_dg40_filter_vs_lr`, `p_dg40_filter_vs_mlp`.

## Claims in the brief

- The filter recovers most of the accuracy that label noise costs a linear model.: narrowed. Holds in three of four noisy cells (66 to 78% of the loss); on breast cancer at 40% noise only about 22% is recovered. Recovery fraction is our own definition.
- Most of the samples it drops are truly mislabeled.: supported. Precision is 65 to 76% with all four tests p < 0.001; a quarter to a third of drops are clean samples.
- It beats a larger model (an MLP) trained on the same noisy labels.: narrowed. True at 20 and 40% noise against an untuned 64-unit MLP; the MLP matches or slightly beats it on clean labels, and no tuned MLP was run.
- It costs little when the labels are clean.: narrowed. Mean drop is 0.1 to 0.2 points and not significant, but 5 seeds cannot prove equivalence, and the filter takes about 5x the fit time.
- The filter needs no clean validation set and no noise-rate estimate.: narrowed. True by construction; robustness to a wrong noise rate or to other noise types was not tested.
- Do not claim it scales to deep networks or large data.: supported. Kept as an explicit limitation.
