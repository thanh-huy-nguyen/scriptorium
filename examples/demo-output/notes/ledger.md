## Claims in the brief

- Label noise hurts simple classifiers.: narrowed. Shown for logistic regression on two small datasets under symmetric noise, not for simple classifiers in general.
- We fit logistic regression with K-fold cross-validation, drop every training sample whose out-of-fold prediction disagrees with its given label, and refit on the rest.: supported. Matches experiments/run.py.
- The filter needs no clean validation set and no noise-rate estimate.: supported. True by design. The number of folds and C are fixed settings.
- The filter recovers most of the accuracy that label noise costs a linear model.: narrowed. Much of the loss is won back on seed means in three of four noisy settings and a small part on breast cancer at 40%. 'More than half' passes a test only on digits at 20%.
- Most of the samples it drops are truly mislabeled.: narrowed. Holds in every noisy setting. False with clean labels, where every dropped sample is correctly labeled.
- It beats a larger model (an MLP) trained on the same noisy labels.: narrowed. True on noisy labels for this one MLP, but plain logistic regression beats it too, so the comparison does not show the value of filtering.
- It costs little when the labels are clean.: supported. Mean accuracy is lower by a fraction of a point on both datasets; equivalence is not claimed.
- This is a small study on two small datasets; do not claim it scales to deep networks or large data.: supported. Only two small datasets, logistic regression and one small MLP were run.
